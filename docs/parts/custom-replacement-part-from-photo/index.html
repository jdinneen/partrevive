Why it breaks

Plastic parts break, and the maker often no longer sells them, or sells only a whole assembly. A cheap clip then looks like a reason to throw a good machine away.

OEM situation

Typically the choices are an expensive original, a used part with an unknown fit, or no part at all. A printed part sits in between: made to order from your photo, often for less than an original assembly.

What we make

This is the main service. You photograph the part, we reply within 24 hours with a yes, a no, or a question. We then design it, print it in the right material (ASA for sun and heat, PA-CF for high load, TPU for flexible, PETG for general use), and ship it. Parts must be under 250 mm and plastic. We do not make food-contact parts, safety-critical parts such as brakes, battery contacts or child items, or parts under high heat unless ASA suits the job.

How to order

  1. Photograph the broken part, and the place it fits, from several sides, with a coin or ruler in the picture. Add the brand, model and any number stamped on the part.
  2. Send the photos on the request page. We reply within 24 hours on business days with a quote, or an honest no if the part is a poor fit for printing.
  3. Approve the quote, and we design and print the part, usually in 5 to 10 business days after approval, then ship it.

Frequently asked questions

What can you print?

Plastic parts under 250 mm in size: knobs, clips, latches, bins, brackets and trim. We use PETG, ASA, PA-CF or TPU. We do not print food-contact or safety-critical parts.

How does the quote work?

You send photos and we reply within 24 hours on business days with a price and a timeline. The quote covers design time for simple parts. Complex or multi-part designs cost more and we tell you before you approve.

Is a printed part as strong as the original?

We do not claim it matches the original. Printed PETG or ASA is strong in most directions, but a layer-line direction exists: the part is weakest where layers pull apart. We orient the print so the main load runs along the layers, and we tell you if a part is a poor candidate. We choose PA-CF where high load is expected.

How do I measure or photograph the part?

Photograph the part from the top, side and mounting end, and the place it fits on a plain background with a coin or ruler beside it, from the top, the side and the mounting end. Also send the model number, usually on a sticker inside the door, under the unit or on the product label. If you still have a broken piece, photograph that too.

What will you refuse to make?

Food-contact items, anything safety-critical such as brakes, battery contacts or child items, and parts that face high heat unless ASA suits the job. We say so in the reply.

What if the part does not fit?

Tell us and we work out why. Accurate photos with a coin for scale cut the risk, and we confirm the details with you before printing.

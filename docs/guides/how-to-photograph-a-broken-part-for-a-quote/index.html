How to photograph a broken part so we can quote it

By PartRevive · 2026-10-21

Short answer: Photograph the broken part from the top, side and mounting end with a coin or ruler beside it, and add the model number. That is usually enough for us to quote within 24 hours on business days.

The short answer

Four things get a quote fast: clear photos from several angles, a coin or ruler for scale, the model number of the machine, and the broken pieces if you still have them. Everything else we can ask about.

What you need

  • A phone camera.
  • A coin or a ruler.
  • A plain, light background such as a sheet of paper.
  • Good light, ideally a window rather than the flash.

Step 1: find the model number

The model number tells us exactly which machine the part belongs to. Look for a sticker or plate in these places.

  • Fridges: inside the fresh-food compartment, on a side wall or near the top.
  • Dryers and dishwashers: inside the door opening or on the back panel.
  • Toolboxes: moulded into the base or on the lid.
  • RV windows: stamped on the frame, or sometimes not marked at all.
  • Cars: the year, model and trim level, which is usually enough.

Photograph the sticker straight on, close enough to read the digits.

Step 2: photograph the part itself

Take at least four photos

  1. Top view: looking straight down, the whole part in frame.
  2. Side view: shows thickness and any hooks or tabs.
  3. Mounting end: the end that attaches to the machine, with holes or clips visible.
  4. The place it fits: the empty spot on the machine, so we see the space it fills.

Always include scale

Put a coin or ruler right beside the part, on the same surface. Tell us which coin, for example a US quarter, which is 24.26 mm wide. A ruler laid flat is even better. A photo without scale forces us to guess, and a guess is how a part ends up too small.

Step 3: add the broken pieces

If the part snapped, keep every piece. Photograph the broken faces, because they show how thick the part was and where it failed. A snap that is flat and clean tells us the load direction, which affects how we print the new one. Do not glue the pieces together before you photograph them.

Step 4: measure the key sizes

If you have a ruler or a pair of calipers, add these notes in your message.

  • The longest length.
  • The width and thickness at the thinnest point.
  • The distance between mounting holes.
  • The diameter of a shaft or pin, if it fits on one.

Round to the nearest millimetre. If you do not have tools, skip this. The scale in the photo is enough for many parts.

Step 5: send it

Use the request page. Include the brand and model, what the part does, and your location for shipping. We reply within 24 hours on business days with a quote, a question, or an honest no. If you approve the quote, printing and shipping usually take 5 to 10 business days.

What we can and cannot make

We print plastic parts up to 250 mm. We do not print food-contact or safety-critical parts, and we do not touch electrical parts. Read the printability checklist if you are unsure. For examples of what a finished quote looks like, see the custom part from a photo page.

If you cannot remove the part

Sometimes the part is stuck, or removing it would break the machine. In that case, photograph it in place from several angles, with the coin held beside it, and measure what you can with a ruler. Tell us if you are unsure about how it comes off. We can often work out the hidden side from the visible one and from other owners' photos of the same model, and we tell you what we cannot see.

If the part is missing entirely

If the part fell off years ago, photograph the empty space and anything that touches it: the pin it sat on, the slot it slid into, the parts either side. Add the model number and a short note on what the part does. A missing part is harder to quote than a broken one, but it is often still possible, and the quote says so plainly.

Send more than you think you need

Extra photos cost you nothing and save a round of questions. If we can quote on the first reply, you get your answer within the 24 hours instead of waiting for a second exchange.

Frequently asked questions

How many photos should I send?

At least four: top, side, mounting end, and the place it fits on the machine. More is fine.

What can I use for scale?

A coin or a ruler placed right beside the part. A US quarter is 24.26 mm wide.

Where is the model number?

On a sticker or plate: inside the door for dryers and dishwashers, inside the compartment for fridges, or on the base of a toolbox.

Should I glue the broken part first?

No. Keep the pieces separate and photograph the broken faces.

What happens after I send the photos?

We reply within 24 hours on business days with a quote, a question, or an honest no.

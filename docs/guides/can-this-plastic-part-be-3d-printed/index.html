Can this broken plastic part be 3D printed? A buyer's checklist

By PartRevive · 2026-10-21

Short answer: Most broken plastic parts under 250 mm can be 3D printed if they are not food-contact, not safety-critical and not exposed to high heat. If the part sits in the sun or heat we use ASA, and for high load we use carbon-fibre nylon (PA-CF).

The short answer

Many broken plastic parts can be 3D printed, but not all. A part is a good candidate when it is small enough, made of plastic, and not asked to do a job where failure hurts someone. This page is the checklist we use before we quote.

Check 1: is it small enough?

Our printers handle parts up to 250 mm in any direction. That covers knobs, clips, latches, hinges, brackets, bins and trim pieces. A large panel or a full shelf is outside that limit, though sometimes it can be made in sections. Measure the longest side with a ruler and tell us.

Check 2: is it plastic?

We print plastic only. A metal part that has a plastic cap or sleeve is fine, because the cap can be printed. Metal gears, springs and screws we do not make. If the original part mixes materials, tell us which piece is broken.

Check 3: how hot does it get?

Heat is the most common reason a printed part fails. Ordinary hobby plastics such as PLA soften in a hot car. We do not use PLA. For parts in the sun, a vehicle or near warm machinery, we print in ASA, which resists UV and heat. If the part touches high heat, such as an oven, a heater or an engine bay, it is not a good candidate, and we say so.

Check 4: what is it carrying?

Strength depends on the load, and on how the part is printed. Printed parts are strong in most directions, but a layer-line direction exists, and a part is weakest where layers pull apart. We orient the print so the main load runs along the layers. We have four materials to match the job.

MaterialBest forNotes
PETGGeneral use, cold, fridge and toolbox partsTough; not for hot places

PA-CF (carbon-fibre nylon)High load, repeated stressStiff and strong; costs more

Check 5: could failure hurt someone?

This is a firm line. We do not print safety-critical parts: brakes, battery contacts, seat belt parts, child items, or anything where a break could injure. We also do not touch electrical parts. We can print the plastic knob or trigger on an appliance, but not a switch or wiring. If you are not sure, ask and we will answer honestly.

Check 6: will it touch food or drink?

We make no food-contact claims. A part that sits where food or drink touches it directly, like a cup or a scoop, is not something we print. Parts such as a fridge door bin that hold packaged items are different, but we still make no certification claims.

What a good candidate looks like

  • A knob, latch, clip or bin under 250 mm.
  • Not near high heat, or in ASA if it is warm.
  • Discontinued or sold only as an expensive assembly.
  • Photographed clearly with a coin or ruler beside it.

Custom parts start from $35, including design time for simple parts. Complex shapes are quoted individually. After you approve the quote, printing and shipping usually take 5 to 10 business days. We quote within 24 hours on business days.

When we say no

We would rather decline than quote a part that will not work. Common reasons we say no: the part is safety-critical, the part must meet a food-contact standard, the part faces an oven or engine bay, the part is bigger than 250 mm, or the original is a metal part we cannot copy in plastic. When we say no, we explain why in a sentence or two, so you can look for another route.

What happens if the part is borderline

Sometimes a part is possible but risky, for example a clip near a warm motor. In that case we tell you the risk, suggest ASA or PA-CF, and let you decide. You are never charged until you approve a quote. A small test print of the critical area is sometimes the cheapest way to check the fit before the whole part is made.

Why photos beat descriptions

A description such as a small white clip with two holes fits a hundred different parts. A photo with a coin beside it fits one. That is why every quote starts with a photo, and why our guide on photographing a broken part is worth a minute of your time.

Frequently asked questions

What is the biggest part you can print?

Up to 250 mm. Larger parts may be possible in sections, which we quote individually.

Can you print a part that gets hot?

In ASA, for moderate heat such as sun or a car interior. We do not print parts for ovens, heaters or engine bays.

Do you print food-contact parts?

No. We make no food-contact claims.

Do you print brakes or safety parts?

No. We do not print safety-critical parts or touch electrical parts.

What does a custom part cost?

From $35, including design time for simple parts. Complex shapes are quoted individually.

How to replace a broken RV screen door latch or handle

By PartRevive · 2026-10-21

Short answer: To replace a broken RV screen door latch, first work out whether it is a left-hand or right-hand door, then measure the distance between the mounting holes. If a stock kit does not match, we print an exact-fit paddle or handle in ASA from a photo, quoted within 24 hours on business days.

The short answer

Most screen door latch failures come down to a cracked plastic paddle, a worn lever or a spring-return part that has given up. Before you buy anything, you need three facts: which hand the door is, how far apart the screw holes sit, and whether the broken part is sold on its own or only inside a full kit.

That last point matters. The paddle and lever can be hard to find on their own, and the small part you need is often not offered alone.

Why screen door latches break

Screen door hardware sits in direct sun and gets used every day. Plastic paddles and levers typically become brittle with UV exposure, then crack at the pivot or where the screw passes through. The spring-return mechanism adds a small, repeated load every time someone walks in or out.

  • A paddle that snaps off at its base is often a sign of aged plastic.
  • A latch that works but will not return is often the spring part, not the handle.
  • Stripped screw holes in thin door skin are a separate problem that a new latch will not fix.

Step 1: identify left hand or right hand

Handedness trips up many first-time buyers. Stand outside the trailer, facing the screen door, and note which side the hinges are on. Many parts are made as a mirrored pair, so a right-hand handle will not work on a left-hand door even though the two look almost identical.

An easy check is to look at which way the paddle or lever points when the latch is at rest and which way you push it. If you are unsure, photograph the door from outside and from inside and we will read the hand from the pictures.

Step 2: measure the hole spacing

Hole spacing is the single most useful measurement. Use a ruler or calipers and measure from the center of one mounting hole to the center of the next. Write the number down in millimeters or inches, and say which you used.

  1. Measure center to center between the two screw holes on the latch body.
  2. Measure the thickness of the door skin or frame where the latch mounts.
  3. Note the screw type and length if you still have the screws.
  4. Check whether the latch also has a strike plate on the frame, and photograph it.

Kit, aftermarket or printed: how they compare

A full kit is the right answer when the whole latch is worn, or when you want everything new at once. A printed part makes sense when only the plastic paddle or lever has failed and the rest of the hardware is sound.

Why we use ASA for a door in the sun

ASA is a plastic made for outdoor use, with good resistance to sun and heat. It suits a screen door latch that lives outside all summer. PETG is also a reasonable choice if the latch will be shaded. We will suggest the better one for your trailer when we quote.

While you are at the door, check the other plastic bits. Step 3: what to photograph

Good photos save a round of emails. Take them with a coin or ruler beside the part, in daylight, with the phone held straight on.

  • The broken latch or handle from the front, with a coin or ruler in view.
  • The back of the latch, showing the mounting holes and any spring.
  • The door from outside, showing the hinge side.
  • Any pieces that broke off, laid flat next to the ruler.
  • The strike plate or catch on the door frame.

Send them on the request page, along with your trailer make and year if you know them. Our guide to photographing a broken part goes into more detail.

Frequently asked questions

How do I tell if my RV screen door is left hand or right hand?

Stand outside facing the door and see which side the hinges are on. If you are unsure, send photos from outside and inside and we will work it out.

What measurement matters most?

The center-to-center distance between the two mounting holes on the latch. Add the door skin thickness if you can.

A zinc kit was listed at $17.09 on Amazon by a third-party seller, and our printed part starts from $22. A kit is the better buy if it fits your hand and holes.

Which material do you use?

Usually ASA, which is made for sun and heat. We may suggest PETG for a shaded door.

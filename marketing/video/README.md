# PartRevive short-video kit

Eight 30-second vertical videos, one per product. Each has its own file in this folder with the script, shot list, captions, spoken lines, titles and hooks. This README is the shooting and posting guide.

Binding rules come from `SCHEMA.md`: no OEM equivalence, no safety or food-contact claims, "from $X" estimates only, lead time "5 to 10 business days after approval", "fits <brand> <model>" wording, no fake reviews or sales counts. "New shop, launched October 2026" is allowed.

## The one rule that matters most

Every part you show must really be printed, and every fit you show must really have been done, before you film. Print first, test-fit, then film. Each video file has a "Parts to print before filming" line. The broken part should be a real broken one. If you break a good part on purpose, put the caption "re-created break" on screen. Never show a fit you have not tried. If a shot did not happen, cut the shot.

## Phone setup in a garage

**Light**
- Open the garage door and film in the daytime if you can; soft shade light beats direct sun.
- Otherwise use two cheap LED panels or desk lamps with daylight bulbs (5000 K to 5600 K), one each side of the bench at 45 degrees, to avoid hard shadows.
- Do not mix warm bulbs and daylight. Turn off the overhead garage light if it is yellow.
- Black plastic loses all detail under a single harsh light. Put a sheet of white paper or a white foam board under and behind the part.

**Framing (9:16)**
- Hold the phone upright (vertical). Shoot 1080 x 1920, 30 fps, rear camera.
- Keep the part in the middle 60 percent of the frame. Leave the top 15 percent and bottom 20 percent clear for captions and platform buttons.
- Plain background: white paper, grey mat, or a clean workbench. Remove clutter and any visible brand logos you do not own.
- Overhead shots: phone on a tripod arm, a stack of books, or a clamp. Use the 1x or 2x lens, not digital zoom beyond 2x.
- Tap to focus on the part and lock exposure. Clean the lens first.
- Always put a US quarter or a ruler next to the part. It shows scale and it matches what we ask customers to do.

**Sound**
- Speak close to the phone in a quiet moment. Turn off the printer fan and air compressor while you talk, or record the voiceover afterward in a closet.
- Say the lines plainly, as written in each file. No hype words, no "OEM quality", no safety claims.

**Length and structure**
- 20 to 40 seconds; the scripts are written for 30. Shorter is fine. Longer than 40 loses viewers.
- One hook in the first 2 seconds, as on-screen text and as a spoken line, over the actual broken part. No logo intro, no "hey guys".
- Beats: hook, broken part, why it breaks, printed part, side by side, fit or install, the ask.
- Cut every pause. Aim for a new shot every 3 to 5 seconds.

**Music**
- Use no music. Natural bench sound plus your voice is enough and it avoids every licensing trap.
- If you want music anyway, use only the platform's own in-app commercial or business sound library (TikTok Commercial Music Library, Instagram "Audio for business accounts", YouTube Audio Library). Do not use trending or popular songs on a business account; they can be muted or claimed. Do not use "royalty free" tracks from random sites without a written licence.

**Captions**
- Burn captions into the video. Use CapCut, DaVinci Resolve, or the YouTube/TikTok caption tools, then export with captions burned in so they look the same on all three platforms.
- White text, black outline or a dark box, at least 48 px, max 2 lines, max about 8 words per line.
- Use the exact caption text in each product file. Check spelling and the numbers (price, 24 hours) before export.
- Add no brand names to the logo, thumbnail art or channel name. Brands appear only in "fits <brand> <model>" text.

**Export**
- 1080 x 1920 MP4 (H.264), under 60 seconds. Keep the same file for all three platforms. Do not upload the TikTok file with the TikTok watermark to the others; export clean from your editor.

## Posting cadence

Three videos per week, for example Monday, Wednesday, Friday, same time each day. Eight videos is about three weeks of posts. After that, repost the best performer with the alternative hook that won the A/B test, or film new parts.

Cross-post each video to all three the same day: YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Instagram Reels. Use the title and description from the product file for YouTube, the caption for TikTok, and the TikTok caption (trimmed to 3 to 5 hashtags) for Instagram.

Suggested order (mix categories so each platform sees variety):
1. Week 1: cooler-lid-latch, dewalt-tstak-latch-clip, rv-window-latch
2. Week 2: whirlpool-kitchenaid-fridge-door-bin, custom-replacement-part-from-photo, dyson-v11-trigger
3. Week 3: rv-screen-door-latch, toyota-tacoma-console-latch, then the best hook rerun

**A/B testing hooks:** each file has 3 alternative hooks. Only change the first 2 seconds. Judge on 3-second hold rate and percent viewed after 48 hours, not on likes. Tracking: note the hook and the numbers in a spreadsheet. Fewer than a few hundred views is noise; do not draw conclusions from it. No made-up results anywhere.

**Reply to comments** within a day. A good question ("does it fit my model?") is the best content: answer with "send a photo and the model number".
